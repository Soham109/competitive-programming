# awoo's Favorite Problem

> [Problem on Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/1697/C)

## Idea

The key observation is that the operations allowed on string $s$ — swapping adjacent "ab" to "ba" and "bc" to "cb" — impose strict positional constraints on the characters 'a' and 'c'. Specifically, 'a' can only move to the right, and 'c' can only move to the left. This means that the relative order of 'a' and 'c' in $s$ must match their order in $t$, and the positions of 'a' and 'c' in $s$ must not violate these movement constraints when aligning with $t$. The character 'b' is flexible and can be freely rearranged between 'a' and 'c'.

## Approach

1. Remove all occurrences of 'b' from both $s$ and $t$, yielding strings $x$ and $y$, respectively. If $x \neq y$, output "NO" because the relative order of 'a' and 'c' cannot be adjusted through the allowed operations.

2. Track the indices of non-'b' characters in $s$ and $t$ using arrays `ps` and `pt`, respectively. This allows us to compare the positions of corresponding 'a' and 'c' characters in both strings.

3. Iterate through the characters in $x$ (or equivalently $y$ since $x = y$). For each character:
   - If the character is 'a', ensure that its index in $s$ (from `ps`) is $\leq$ its corresponding index in $t$ (from `pt`), because 'a' can only move to the right.
   - If the character is 'c', ensure that its index in $s$ is $\geq$ its corresponding index in $t$, because 'c' can only move to the left.

4. If any of the above constraints are violated, output "NO". Otherwise, output "YES".

## Complexity

- **Time:** $O(n)$ per test case, as we iterate through the strings once to filter out 'b' and once to validate the constraints. The total complexity across all test cases is $O(sum of  n)$, which is bounded by $10^5$.
- **Space:** $O(n)$ per test case to store the filtered strings and indices of non-'b' characters.

## Notes

- The removal of 'b' simplifies the problem by reducing it to checking the relative order and movement constraints of 'a' and 'c'. This is the critical insight that makes the problem tractable.
