# Spying on the Beaver

> [Problem on Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/2257/C)

## Idea

The Beaver walks from the root straight down to one dam, so all you ever learn is which cameras he passed. Two dams are indistinguishable exactly when their root paths hit the same set of cameras. That turns into a decision at each vertex on its own. At vertex $u$ the dams underneath split into groups, one per child subtree holding a dam, plus $u$ itself when $u$ is a dam. Those groups have to be told apart and only the edges down to those children can do it. One group can be left without a camera, since seeing nothing at $u$ is itself a signal, but only one. So a vertex with $g$ dam-carrying children needs $g - 1$ cameras, or all $g$ when $u$ is a dam, because then stopping at $u$ has already claimed the silent case.

## Approach

1. Count dams per subtree first. `cnt[u]` starts at $1$ for a dam and picks up its children's counts, and since the input guarantees $p_i < i$, walking `for(int u=n; u>=1; u--)` reaches every child before its parent. The counting is done bottom-up with no recursion at all.

2. Keep only the children that matter. `good` holds the children $v$ with `cnt[v]` above zero, because a subtree with no dam in it is never a possible destination and an edge into it would never tell two dams apart.

3. When `dam[u]` is set, put a camera on every good child. The path that ends at $u$ triggers nothing below $u$, so that signature is taken, and every branch leaving $u$ now needs its own camera to be separated from it and from the others.

4. When $u$ is not a dam, skip `good[0]` and camera the rest. Anything ending in that first subtree is recognised by the absence of the other cameras, so it costs nothing, while a second uncovered branch would be confused with it.

5. Those two rules are enough to separate every pair. Take dams $a \neq b$ and let $u$ be the deepest vertex their paths share. Either they head into different good children of $u$, and at least one of the two carries a camera which fires for one and not the other, or one of them is $u$ itself, in which case $u$ is a dam and step 3 has already covered every branch below it.

## Complexity

- **Time:** $O(n)$ per test case — every vertex and every edge is touched a constant number of times.
- **Space:** $O(n)$ for the children lists, the subtree counts, and the answer.

## Notes

- Each camera is reported as the lower endpoint $u$ of the edge to `p[u]`, which is the format the problem asks for. Any minimum set is accepted, so the order they come out in does not matter.
- Doing this with an explicit downward loop instead of a DFS keeps a path-shaped tree with $n = 10^5$ from overflowing the call stack.
- `cnt[u]` is only ever compared against zero, so nothing overflows even though it is summing subtree sizes.
