# Pursuit

> [Problem on Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/1530/C)

## Idea

Out of $m$ rounds played, only the best $k = m - \lfloor m/4 \rfloor$ of them count toward the score. In every extra round we take $100$ and the opponent takes $0$, so an extra round always lands among our best and never among theirs. There is only one thing to decide, which is how many extra rounds to play, so we can just try $0$, then $1$, then $2$, and stop at the first one that works. To make each try cheap, sort both score lists from high to low and keep prefix sums, so the sum of anyone's best $j$ rounds is one lookup.

## Approach

1. Sort `a` and `b` from high to low. The macro `all(c)` expands to `(c).rbegin(), (c).rend()`, so `sort(all(a))` sorts in decreasing order. The prefix sums `pa` and `pb` then hold the sum of the top $j$ scores in `pa[j]`, ready in $O(1)$.

2. Pick a number of extra rounds $x$ and work out how many rounds count. With `total` $= n + x$ rounds played in all, the number that count is `k = total - total / 4`, which is the $m - \lfloor m/4 \rfloor$ from the statement.

3. Every new round gives us $100$, the highest score possible, so none of them can be dropped. They take up $x$ of the $k$ counted slots and the slots left over go to our best old rounds, which is why `oldA = k - x` and `sa = 100 * x + pa[oldA]`.

4. The opponent scores $0$ in each new round, so those rounds sit at the bottom of their list and get dropped first. They are left with their own best rounds, `oldB = min(k, n)`. The $\min$ is there because $k$ grows past $n$ once $x$ is large, and they only have $n$ rounds to give.

5. Start at $x = 0$, go up one at a time, and print the first $x$ where `sa >= sb`. Since the values are tried in order, the first one that works is the smallest one that works.

## Complexity

- **Time:** $O(n \log n)$ per test case — the two sorts are the expensive part, since each step of the $x$ loop is $O(1)$ and the loop ends within $O(n)$ steps.
- **Space:** $O(n)$ for the two sorted arrays and their prefix sums.

## Notes

- The `for (int x = 0; ; x++)` loop has no stopping condition of its own, so it is worth checking that it ends. Each $b_i \leq 100$ means `sb` is at most $100n$, while `sa` is at least $100x$, so by $x = n$ the test has passed at the latest.
- That same bound keeps the indexing safe. For $x \leq n$ we have $k \geq \frac{3}{4}(n + x)$, so `oldA` $= k - x \geq \frac{n}{2} \geq 0$ and `pa[oldA]` never reads outside the array.
- Plain `int` is enough here. No score is above $100$, so no prefix sum gets past $100n$.
