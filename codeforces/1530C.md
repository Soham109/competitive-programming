# Pursuit

> [Problem on Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/1530/C)

## Idea

Only the best $k = m - \lfloor m/4 \rfloor$ rounds count, where $m$ is the total number of rounds played. Every extra round is worth $100$ to us and $0$ to the opponent, so an extra round is always among our best and never among theirs. That makes the whole thing a one-dimensional search over the number of extra rounds $x$, and once both arrays are sorted in decreasing order a prefix sum answers "sum of my best $j$ rounds" instantly.

## Approach

1. Sort `a` and `b` in decreasing order. The macro `all(c)` expands to `(c).rbegin(), (c).rend()`, so `sort(all(a))` is a descending sort, and the prefix sums `pa`, `pb` then give the sum of the top $j$ scores as `pa[j]` in $O(1)$.

2. Fix a candidate $x$ and derive the count of scored rounds from it. With `total` $= n + x$ rounds played, the number that count is `k = total - total / 4`, which is exactly $m - \lfloor m/4 \rfloor$ from the statement.

3. All $x$ new rounds award us the maximum $100$, so none of them can be cut. They occupy $x$ of the $k$ counted slots and the remaining slots go to our strongest old rounds, hence `oldA = k - x` and `sa = 100 * x + pa[oldA]`.

4. The opponent scores $0$ in every new round, so those rounds sit at the bottom of their list and never survive the cut. Their total stays the sum of their own best rounds, `oldB = min(k, n)`, and the $\min$ matters because $k$ can exceed $n$ once $x$ is large.

5. Scan $x$ upward from $0$ and print the first value with `sa >= sb`. Minimality needs no monotonicity argument, it follows from testing the candidates in increasing order.

## Complexity

- **Time:** $O(n \log n)$ per test case — the two sorts dominate, since the scan over $x$ costs $O(1)$ per step and stops after $O(n)$ steps.
- **Space:** $O(n)$ for the two sorted arrays and their prefix sums.

## Notes

- The `for (int x = 0; ; x++)` loop has no bound, and the safety net is arithmetic. Each $b_i \leq 100$ gives `sb` $\leq 100n$, while `sa` $\geq 100x$, so the condition fires by $x = n$ at the latest.
- That bound also protects the indexing. For $x \leq n$ we have $k \geq \frac{3}{4}(n + x)$, so `oldA` $= k - x \geq \frac{n}{2} \geq 0$ and `pa[oldA]` never reads out of range.
- Plain `int` is enough throughout. Every score is at most $100$, so no prefix sum exceeds $100n$.
